## Signal 01 — Dependence enters the local window

**What changed.** A locally stationary process has a generating parameter that changes gradually with rescaled time. The paper estimates it by minimizing a kernel-weighted local contrast. For causal processes with potentially infinite memory, it proves pointwise and uniform consistency and, under stronger smoothness conditions, a pointwise Gaussian limit.

**Why it matters.** Yesterday's accepted result assumes independent regression observations around a fixed point. This paper allows serial dependence and a moving target, closer to what residual updates can create. The bridge remains incomplete because an intrinsic-mode recursion also changes its neighborhoods and data.

**Evidence.** [Primary paper, p. 7, 8](/sources#src-external-arxiv-2005-07397v2).

**Confidence.** High for the theorem statements and their stated conditions, which are explicit on pages 7-8.

**Assumptions and limitations.** The process satisfies the paper's contraction, local-stationarity, moment, identifiability, and bandwidth conditions.; The target is a time-varying parameter, not an intrinsic mode or recursive reference. Limitations: The asymptotic-normality rate is suboptimal without additional bias conditions.; No guarantee is transferred to adaptive or robust recursion.

## Signal 02 — Robustness stops at consistency

**What changed.** For a time-varying autoregression, the paper studies a localized least-absolute-value contrast, a loss less sensitive to extreme residuals than squared error. It proves uniform consistency with a finite first moment, but does not carry its general asymptotic-normality theorem into this nondifferentiable example.

**Why it matters.** That split sharpens the Residual's target question. Dependence and time variation can be handled theoretically, and a robust contrast can be consistent, but the paper does not jointly supply bounded-score contamination control, uncertainty calibration, and recursive residual stability.

**Evidence.** [Primary paper, p. 9, 10](/sources#src-external-arxiv-2005-07397v2).

**Confidence.** High for the contrast and consistency boundary because both are explicit on pages 9-10.

**Assumptions and limitations.** The coefficient stays inside the paper's contraction region and changes locally smoothly.; Robustness means the paper's least-absolute-value example, not an epsilon-contamination guarantee. Limitations: No bounded influence function, contamination fraction, or recursive target is analyzed.; Only consistency is established for the nondifferentiable robust loss.

## Signal 03 — The experiments test estimation, not contamination

**What changed.** Three 1000-replication studies cover time-varying GARCH, infinite-memory ARCH, and integer-valued ARCH processes. Their tables report smaller root mean integrated squared error at larger displayed sample sizes; a stock-index application tracks changing volatility.

**Why it matters.** The experiments show that local parameter tracking is computationally concrete across several dependent models. They do not validate the paper's robust least-absolute-value case, inject outliers, or measure error propagation through repeated intrinsic-mode residualization.

**Evidence.** [Primary paper, p. 21, 23](/sources#src-external-arxiv-2005-07397v2).

**Confidence.** High for the table trends; a prose/Table 1 sample-size mismatch is preserved as a limitation.

**Assumptions and limitations.** The simulations use the paper's Gaussian quasi-maximum-likelihood estimator, fixed bandwidth rule, and stated kernels.; The measured targets are model parameters rather than decomposed signal components. Limitations: There is no contamination stress test or robust-loss comparison.; The real-data application is descriptive and does not establish causal or recursive-filter performance.

## Why this matters

The distinct advance is a more realistic local setting: observations can be dependent, memory can be long, and the target can drift. This gives the IMF project a single-stage benchmark for asking whether each residual resembles a locally stationary process. It does not make recursive residuals satisfy the paper's conditions automatically. The next experiment should test those conditions stage by stage, compare squared and robust losses against one target, and check whether calibration survives recursion.

## Unresolved question

After each intrinsic-mode residual update, can the resulting dependent, time-varying noise be shown to satisfy a locally stationary model strongly enough to calibrate a robust local estimator at the next stage?
