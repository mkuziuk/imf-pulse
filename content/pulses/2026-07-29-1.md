## Signal 01 — A stronger smoothness regime recovers the canonical bandwidth scale

**What changed.** With a second-order bias expansion, the selected bandwidth converges to the deterministic asymptotic optimum, whose variance-versus-squared-bias balance gives an n^(-1/5) scale.

**Why it matters.** The selector does more than achieve the right loss: under stronger conditions it identifies the same bandwidth scale that an oracle bias-variance calculation would choose.

**Evidence.** [Primary paper, p. 8, 9](/sources#src-external-arxiv-1705-10046v1).

**Confidence.** High: Theorems 3.8 and 3.9 state the approximation and bandwidth ratio explicitly.

**Assumptions and limitations.** The parameter curve is twice continuously differentiable.; The kernel is symmetric.; The objective and stationary approximation admit the specified second derivatives.; The bias term is nondegenerate. Limitations: These assumptions are stronger than those for basic asymptotic optimality.; The convergence distribution and rate of the selected bandwidth remain open.

## Signal 02 — The guarantee targets predictive model distance

**What changed.** The paper proves that a quasi-leave-one-out bandwidth selector asymptotically matches the best admissible bandwidth for averaged or integrated Fisher-information-weighted error, interpreted as a Kullback–Leibler-type distance.

**Why it matters.** This moves bandwidth choice for a broad class of dependent, evolving processes from a model-by-model tuning recipe to a general criterion with a precise optimality target.

**Evidence.** [Primary paper, p. 7, 8](/sources#src-external-arxiv-1705-10046v1).

**Confidence.** High: this is the formal content of Theorem 3.6, not an inference from simulations.

**Assumptions and limitations.** Local stationary approximation by a smooth parameter curve.; Polynomially decaying functional dependence.; Identification, curvature, differentiability, and martingale-score conditions.; Admissible kernel, weight, and bandwidth set. Limitations: The loss is Fisher-information weighted and prediction-oriented.; The theorem is asymptotic and relative to the bandwidth search set.

## Signal 03 — Misspecification evidence is encouraging, not a robustness theorem

**What changed.** Across 2,000-replication simulations with sample sizes 200 and 500, the selector was compared with asymptotic and realization-specific oracle choices for four model families; separate runs fitted a tvAR model to tvMA and tvARCH data.

**Why it matters.** The experiments probe the practical failure mode readers care about—using the procedure when the process family or innovation distribution is wrong—but only as finite-sample evidence.

**Evidence.** [Primary paper, p. 14, 15, 17](/sources#src-external-arxiv-1705-10046v1).

**Confidence.** Medium: the design is explicit, but the prose reports mainly qualitative comparisons and does not supply a numerical table.

**Assumptions and limitations.** The selected sinusoidal parameter curves and four process families represent the relevant use cases.; The weighted distance is an appropriate evaluation target.; The unreported random seeds do not materially distort the Monte Carlo summary. Limitations: Misspecification performance is not covered by the stated theorem.; Some alternative innovation scenarios violate theoretical moment conditions.; The experiment covers only two sample sizes and selected models.

## Why this matters

The paper clarifies a subtle point that is often lost in the phrase “adaptive bandwidth”: the selector is adaptive to an evolving dependent process, but its proved target is a weighted model-distance criterion, not raw parameter-curve error. That distinction makes the result more useful, not less—it tells practitioners when cross-validation is choosing for predictive fit, identifies the assumptions that make the guarantee credible, and prevents favorable misspecification simulations from being mistaken for a theorem.

## Unresolved question

Can one construct a stable local adaptive selector that is asymptotically optimal for an unweighted quadratic error on the parameter curve—especially when different parameter curves have different smoothness—and establish a convergence rate or limit distribution for its selected bandwidth?
