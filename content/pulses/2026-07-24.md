## Signal 01 — The recursion now turns a corner

**What changed.** MdMvFIF treats a data cube as two coupled decomposition problems. It first estimates a spatial support shared across time slices, extracts a spatial IMF with FFT-accelerated iterative filtering, subtracts it, then estimates a temporal filter from rotations of the spatial state and extracts a temporal IMF. The outer loop repeats while oscillations remain in either direction.

**Why it matters.** This is more than applying a one-dimensional filter channel by channel. The residual passed to the temporal step has already been altered by the spatial step, so ordering, stopping rules, and support selection become part of the estimand. That architecture gives the IMF project a concrete external example where recursive error can propagate across directions as well as stages.

**Evidence.** [Primary paper, p. 4, 5](/sources#src-external-arxiv-2412-00553v1).

**Confidence.** High for the stated algorithmic structure; it is explicit in the method prose and pseudocode.

**Assumptions and limitations.** The paper's periodic-boundary convention and adaptive support rules are retained.; The claim describes the preprint algorithm rather than a convergence theorem for every data cube. Limitations: The preprint does not isolate the effect of reversing the spatial and temporal order.; Its stopping thresholds remain implementation choices.

## Signal 02 — Adaptive is not yet robust

**What changed.** The paper broadens iterative filtering to signals varying simultaneously in space and time and demonstrates the method on two synthetic constructions plus a 48 by 97 by 365 air-temperature cube. Yet each inner step is still a convolutional moving average. No bounded-score loss, explicit contamination model, or outlier stress test is introduced.

**Why it matters.** That distinction keeps two meanings from collapsing. MdMvFIF is nonlinear because its supports and lengths are data-adaptive; IRMF in this project is robust because the local estimator changes. Combining them would require replacing or robustifying both directional fits, then checking whether contamination rejected in the spatial pass leaks into the temporal residual—or the reverse.

**Evidence.** [Primary paper, p. 3, 6, 8](/sources#src-external-arxiv-2412-00553v1).

**Confidence.** High for the reported estimator and example inventory; moderate for conclusions about behavior under contamination, which the paper does not test.

**Assumptions and limitations.** Robustness is interpreted as resistance to contaminated observations, not general versatility.; Only arXiv version 1 was inspected. Limitations: The examples are demonstrations rather than a systematic uncertainty study.; The absence of a contamination experiment is not evidence that MdMvFIF fails under contamination.

## Why this matters

The interesting bridge is architectural. The local IMF work asks how sequential residualization changes error across scale; MdMvFIF adds a second sequence, alternating direction as well as scale. That suggests an experiment more informative than simply porting code: compare space-first and time-first decompositions under clean noise and localized impulsive contamination, using the same clean same-method target in both orders. Any difference would identify order sensitivity before a full robust extension is attempted.

## Unresolved question

If bounded-score local fits replace both moving averages, can an alternating space-time IRMF control contamination without making the recovered components depend strongly on which direction is filtered first?
